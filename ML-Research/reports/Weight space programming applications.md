# Weight-Space Programming Compiles and Repairs, Rarely Teaches

Weight-space programming (WSP) is a real but narrow capability, and the evidence so far shows two things it does well. First, a frontier LLM can **compile a short, stateable algorithm into a small network**: MinAtar and Go policies of 17K–1.5M weights. Second, it can **find and remove a low-rank obstacle in an existing network**: the looping direction in Qwen3.5-0.8B, and the ramp stall in a Kinetix PPO actor. It has not shown that it can add a capability the network lacks. It also has not shown that it can read behavior from the weights of anything larger than a toy. So WSP is most likely to beat existing methods in five places. The first is repairing known failure modes in small open models, as an automated front end to direction ablation. The second is debugging and patching small deployed RL policies. The third is generating ground-truth circuits for interpretability research and teaching. The fourth is small controllers that need to be auditable or verifiable. The fifth is triage and red-teaming inside activation-based audit pipelines. It is unlikely to displace Adam or PPO, MEMIT-class knowledge editors, robust unlearning, same-architecture merging, equivariant meta-networks, or search-based game engines. The independent audits sharpen this picture. Every released artifact reproduces bit-exact, but every headline shrinks against a fair baseline. The Qwen base score of 7.9% on MATH-500 comes from a looping decoding regime; with thinking turned off, the unedited model scores about 44–52%. A textbook difference-of-means direction recovers 79% of Astra's gain. Tuned PPO beats Astra about 2× on Seaquest. And the only independent live re-run of the Qwen process scored 12.9%, not 48% ([Zhang](https://github.com/ZhangYiqun018/rethinking-weight-space-programming/blob/main/results/CLAIMS.md); [Toledo](https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs/issues/4); [OpenWSP](https://github.com/openwsp/openwsp)). One rule organizes the whole map. WSP wins when the solution has a **short verbal description** and data or rollouts are expensive. It loses when the solution is a **statistical compression of data**.

## Three days of audits shrank every headline, but none vanished

The post by Takehi, Teoh, Zhang and Khattab defines WSP as a general-purpose LLM that "reads and writes a network's weights directly", with no optimizer and no gradient descent, working from a natural-language spec ([post](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/)). Its eight experiments mix four regimes. Three (Exps 1, 2 and 4) give the model raw weights as text with no tools. One (Exp 3) adds Python over the weights. Two (Exps 6 and 8) add Python plus a budget of up to 50 forward passes. One (Exp 7) uses an attention-pattern tool, and Exp 5 is the zero-shot MinAtar writing task. Matthew Honnibal's main objection is that "just by looking at the raw weights" literally describes only the three no-tools experiments, which involve 17K–172K weights. Every language-model result used Python, and the Qwen result also ran the model ([Honnibal](https://honnibal.dev/blog/astra-read-write-misleading)). The authors deserve credit for releasing complete logs and bit-exact diffs, which let outsiders audit the work within 72 hours. The table below sets each claim beside what survived that audit.

| Experiment | Original claim | Independent finding | What survives |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exp 8: Qwen3.5-0.8B math | MATH-500 7.9 → 48.1 after editing 0.016% of weights (50 forward passes) | Re-evaluations of the released edit give 50.6–51.0%. With thinking off, the base already scores 44–52%. A difference-of-means direction alone gets 41.9%. Only 1 of 14 thinking-on runs produced the big gain. A live re-run got 12.9% | A real loop fix, about 8.8 points better than the textbook recipe. Not a reasoning gain |
| Exp 5: MinAtar from rules | Breakout 517 vs PPO-500M 25.3 | Tuned PPO gets 56–440 on Breakout and 99–114 on Seaquest (about 2× Astra). 51.6% of Breakout episodes hit the step cap. Without the game's source code, Seaquest falls to 0.8 | Genuine program compilation. A 20× win only on the game with a closed-form solution |
| Exp 6: 9×9 Go | Beats an equal-sized PPO net after 50 passes | A 30-line tactics script beats the same PPO nets by more than Astra's 0-pass net does. Astra's nets beat the script head-to-head 90–95% | Encodes real Go tactics. Says nothing about absolute strength |
| Exp 4: Kinetix repair | 0/256 → 1,024/1,024 after editing 3.5% of weights, in one reply | A 2-parameter open-loop controller matches the edit on most of the perturbation grid. One edited weight goes from 0.095 to about 876. OpenWSP's re-run failed | The most novel RL result, but a single scene and a single run |

Sources for the table: [post](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/); [Zhang CLAIMS](https://github.com/ZhangYiqun018/rethinking-weight-space-programming/blob/main/results/CLAIMS.md); [ML Intern rebuild](https://huggingface.co/ML-Intern-lab/Qwen3.5-0.8B-weight-space-programming); [weight-space-horizon](https://github.com/hamley241/weight-space-horizon); [issue #8](https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs/issues/8); [OpenWSP](https://github.com/openwsp/openwsp).

Two details from the audits matter for every application below. The Qwen edit does not "subtract" the loop direction, as the post says. Toledo re-derived it from the bytes as **W = W₀ − 6·outer(u, mask·(uᵀW₀))**, which reverses and amplifies the component sixfold. The dose of 6 was chosen on practice answers, not on test problems ([Toledo](https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs/issues/4)). The capability is also frontier-only for now. On the game-identification task, DeepSeek V4.1 and Kimi K3 confidently named a game for randomly initialized weights, while Astra abstained 12 times out of 12 ([Zhang blog](https://zhangyiqun018.github.io/rethinking-weight-space-programming/)). An independent MinAtar re-run found the same split: DeepSeek models scored near random, while Astra spent about **$12.28 for 9 attempts** ([yzw-22 repro](https://github.com/yzw-22/astra-weight-space-repro)). Process reproducibility remains the open question. The published Seaquest network was the luckiest draw: only 1 of 10 fresh replies reached 90% of its score ([Zhang CLAIMS](https://github.com/ZhangYiqun018/rethinking-weight-space-programming/blob/main/results/CLAIMS.md)).

## Short programs beat gradients; statistical compression beats LLMs

The cleanest way to predict where WSP applies is to split it into three separable capabilities and ask which prior each one exploits. **Compile** means writing a network from an informal algorithm (MinAtar, Go). **Repair** means localized edits to a trained network (Kinetix, Qwen). **Read** means classifying or explaining a network from its weights (Exps 1–3 and 7). An LLM searches over programs that are short in English or Python. Gradient descent is biased toward functions that are simple in *parameter space* ([Valle-Pérez et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08522)). Gradient descent soaks up high-entropy regularities in data that have no short verbal description. The MinAtar split shows this exactly. Breakout has a closed-form intercept-the-ball policy, and Astra's network is close to a hand-written lookahead heuristic that scores 546. Seaquest has no compact algorithm, and tuned PPO doubles Astra's score there ([Zhang CLAIMS](https://github.com/ZhangYiqun018/rethinking-weight-space-programming/blob/main/results/CLAIMS.md)). The authors' own Gemma 3 270M null result fits the same frame. Editing "could not add an ability the model lacked": MATH-500 moved only from 6.8 to at most 7.7 ([post](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/)).

The compile mode belongs in a known lineage. Humans hand-set weights. **Tracr** compiles a formal DSL (RASP) into transformer weights that are correct by construction ([Tracr](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05062)). **COMPNET** learned a compiler from C programs to surrogate-network weights ([Weber et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.15078)). WSP moves this one step further: the source language is informal intent, the compiler is a general LLM, and correctness is checked only by testing. That trade gives up Tracr's guarantees and determinism. In return it removes the DSL ceiling: Go liberties are not naturally expressible in RASP. The repair mode is equally familiar. The Qwen fix is mechanically single-direction ablation baked into MLP output weights, the operation that Arditi et al. showed controls refusal in 13 chat models up to 72B ([Arditi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717)). Low intrinsic dimensionality explains why such tiny edits can matter. About 200 parameters in a random subspace recover 90% of RoBERTa's fine-tuning performance on MRPC ([Aghajanyan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)).

The map below applies that frame to each area. It combines the verdicts from all five research threads.

## Repairing known failure modes is the first real application

The strongest near-term case is repair: finding the one component that blocks an ability the network already has, then cutting it out. In Qwen3.5-0.8B, 85 of the first 96 practice answers hit the 32,768-token limit. After the 50-pass edit, the token-limit rate fell from **93.0% to 33.1%**, and IFEval rose from 21.0 to 62.3 ([post](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/)). The arithmetic shows this is unblocking, not new reasoning. Accuracy tracks almost exactly the share of answers that finish. Toledo's summary is that "the edit recovers what the recommended settings give": under the model card's non-thinking settings, the unedited model scores 45.6–51.7% with almost no truncation ([Toledo](https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs/issues/4)). Research on why reasoning models loop supports a one-direction fix and also predicts that it stays partial. Pipis et al. trace looping to learned bias toward an easy cyclic action, not to decoding. Distilled students loop most, with OpenThinker3-1.5B looping in **30%** of greedy responses ([Pipis et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12895)). A third of Qwen's answers still loop after the edit, which fits that mechanism.

Repair competes with tools that are cheap, closed-form and already automated. Abliteration orthogonalizes weights against a known direction. In a cross-tool comparison, single-pass abliteration preserved capability better than an optimization-based alternative ([arXiv 2512.13655](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13655v2)). Steer2Edit converts steering vectors into training-free rank-1 edits on individual heads and neurons. It already reports **−12.2% reasoning length** at matched performance, a direct precedent for editing weights to fix over-long reasoning ([Steer2Edit](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09870)). Persona vectors locate sycophancy, "evil" and hallucination directions in 7–8B models. Anthropic found that preventive steering during fine-tuning costs little-to-no MMLU, while steering after the fact costs capability ([persona vectors](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.21509)). The agent's measurable contribution is therefore narrow. Zhang's no-LLM difference-of-means control gets **41.9%** on MATH-500, and Astra's edit beats that recipe by **+8.8 points [+6.6, +10.8]**. Random-direction controls stay at 5–8% ([Zhang CLAIMS](https://github.com/ZhangYiqun018/rethinking-weight-space-programming/blob/main/results/CLAIMS.md)). WSP's value here is choosing which layers and columns to edit, setting the dose, and checking side effects within a small forward-pass budget. It does not replace the ablation math. The ceiling comes from how the behavior is represented. Refusal and persona traits look one-dimensional. Other work argues that refusal lives in multi-dimensional "concept cones", and that any refusal direction trades off over-refusal along the same knob ([arXiv 2602.02132](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02132v1)).

The RL counterpart is the Kinetix car. Official PPO trained for 1B steps never cleared the ramp. Astra's one-reply edit added a small side path that reverses the car, then switches off and returns control to the trained policy ([post](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/)). Nothing standard performs a one-shot, zero-rollout, localized patch of a trained actor. Exploration methods and curricula need large amounts of environment interaction. OpenAI Five's "surgery" preserved function across architecture changes during a 10-month run without adding behavior ([Berner et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.06680)). The honest competitor is simpler than either. A two-parameter open-loop controller (reverse for 16 steps, then drive) matches the edit across most perturbations. The edit wins only at the edges, such as friction ×2.0, where it succeeds 16/16 against 0/16 ([Zhang CLAIMS](https://github.com/ZhangYiqun018/rethinking-weight-space-programming/blob/main/results/CLAIMS.md)). In practice, WSP repair amounts to compiling a JSRL-style opening guide into the actor. What it buys is a single artifact with no runtime branching. That matters for fixed-shape NN runtimes, fielded robots and game AI, where shipping a code override is awkward. Two risks need attention: regressions on other scenes, which nobody measured, and edit magnitude. One Kinetix weight goes from about 0.095 to **about 876**, so "3.5% of weights" understates how much the network changed ([issue #8](https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs/issues/8)). The third-party WSPBench offers early evidence that weight access helps repair. GPT-6.1 Sol fixed **14/32 planted faults with behavior probes alone and 30/32 with probes plus weights**, and left all 8 clean controls intact ([WSPBench](https://github.com/alexcarterhq/wspbench)).

## Editing, unlearning and merging mostly favor existing linear algebra

Knowledge editing is where the WSP pitch fits worst. ROME and MEMIT already write the least-squares-optimal low-rank update for "change this key's value, preserve these other keys". AlphaEdit projects updates into the null space of preserved knowledge and beats MEMIT across several thousand sequential edits ([AlphaEdit](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02355v4)). The failures that remain are capacity and interference limits in a shared subspace, not poor authorship of individual edits. A 2026 reproducibility study finds AlphaEdit's protection "bounded rather than unconditional" at higher edit counts ([arXiv 2606.26783](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.26783)). BetaEdit claims to stay effective after 10,000 edits, a range where other editors collapse to near-zero efficacy ([BetaEdit](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2026/0508.pdf)). An LLM reasoning over weights can at best rediscover these solves. WSP's demonstrated mode, removing a direction, is also the opposite of knowledge editing, which inserts a new association. The real opening is orchestration. Parametric editors score **under 50%** on RippleEdits' related facts that should change along with the edited one ([RippleEdits](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.12976)). An agent that generates the ripple set, picks layers and verifies with forward passes could call ROME or AlphaEdit as tools. That is an editing agent, not weight-space programming.

Unlearning flips WSP from defender to attacker. On Zephyr-7B, RMU drops WMDP-Bio from 64.4 to 29.9. Orthogonalization, the very operation the Qwen edit performs, restores it to **64.7**, and fine-tuning on just 10 unrelated examples brings it back to 61.6 ([Łucki et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.18025v4)). Their conclusion is that current unlearning "primarily obscures" knowledge rather than erasing it, and a WSP direction subtraction is just one more obscuring edit. "Watch the Weights" shows that weight-derived directions can detect inference on erased topics with up to **95.42%** accuracy and can steer a model to recover "unlearned" information ([WeightWatch](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00161v2)). The application that follows is auditing and red-teaming unlearning claims before release, not performing unlearning. Robust erasure needs knowledge destroyed across many weights. That is the dense, distributed case in which WSP did nothing on Gemma.

Merging splits in two. Same-architecture merging is already cheap, mostly data-free, and well tuned by search. Sakana's evolutionary merge lifted MGSM-JA from about 30 for the best source model to **52.0** in parameter space and **55.2** with layer routing ([Akiba et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.13187v2)). An LLM author has to beat CMA-ES over a fitness function. It can do that only when evaluations are expensive enough that its prior saves many of them. Diagnosing interference is a better fit. Souper-Model found sign conflicts in **52.99%** of active parameters and severe regressions from TIES and DARE in domains like tool use ([Souper-Model](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.13254)). The authors' "true cross-architecture merging by grafting circuits" targets the hardest open problem. The best 2026 method, Transport and Merge, aligns neurons by optimal transport on about 2,000 inputs per dataset. It gains only 0.1–2 points on most averages, for example Indonesian 38.43 → 39.18 ([Transport and Merge](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05495)). Grafting requires the capability to be localized and an aligned interface between residual streams. The second requirement needs activation data, and no amount of reading weights substitutes for it.

The "zero-data steering" pitch also has established rivals that the post never benchmarks. Text-to-LoRA maps a task description to a Mistral-7B adapter zero-shot, though task-specific adapters still win ([T2L](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2506.06105)). Drag-and-Drop LLMs generate full LoRA sets in seconds with "up to 12,000× lower overhead than full fine-tuning" ([DnD](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.16406)). Calling the Qwen fix "zero-data" is also misleading. It needed 50 passes over up to 512 problems each, and at 10 or 25 passes the token-limit rate barely moved, from 93.0% to 87.2% and 86.4% ([post](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/)).

## Reading weights works on toys and fails on behavior

The read side has the most impressive anecdotes and the weakest scaling story. In Exp 3, Astra found exactly the ground-truth induction heads in 7.16M-parameter character transformers by finding a previous-token head and then taking weight-matrix products ([post](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/)). That is K-composition analysis from Elhage et al.'s framework. The framework is fully weight-based: QK and OV circuits, Frobenius composition scores, and eigenvalue checks for copying ([Elhage et al.](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html)). Zhang describes it as Astra "correctly applies a known interpretability recipe without being told it" ([Zhang CLAIMS](https://github.com/ZhangYiqun018/rethinking-weight-space-programming/blob/main/results/CLAIMS.md)). An agent that runs Elhage-style playbooks automatically over many heads and layers is a credible tool. Even the game-identification result has a deflating baseline, though. A 20-line, three-rule script with no LLM gets 3/4, and under anonymization Astra's answers match the script's pattern exactly ([Zhang CLAIMS](https://github.com/ZhangYiqun018/rethinking-weight-space-programming/blob/main/results/CLAIMS.md)).

Beyond canonical circuits, reading breaks down. At 1.5M weights, fresh blind Astra sessions swapped the 0-pass and 50-pass Go networks 3/3 times at ≥94% confidence. Their behavioral predictions were no better than a control given no weights (capture-rate MAE 31 vs 28 points) ([Zhang CLAIMS](https://github.com/ZhangYiqun018/rethinking-weight-space-programming/blob/main/results/CLAIMS.md)). On Gemma 3 270M, Astra found many weight differences between the honest and sycophantic fine-tunes but could not connect them to behavior in any of 4 attempts. Given attention patterns on probe texts it wrote itself, it succeeded 3/3. In head 10.2, the sycophant put **85.7%** of its attention on the user's suggested answer, compared with 0.75% in the honest model ([post](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/)). The broader field reached the same conclusion earlier. Anthropic reports that global, prompt-independent weight connections are less interpretable than per-prompt attribution graphs because of **weight interference** ([Anthropic](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/methods.html?slug=calc-36-plus-59)). Superposition means individual neurons are not the units of meaning ([Toy Models of Superposition](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/toy_model/index.html)).

Auditing benchmarks set the bar that weight reading would have to clear. On AuditBench's 56 Llama-3.3-70B targets with implanted behaviors, a default chat agent succeeds about 37% of the time, and scaffolded black-box tools exceed 50%. White-box activation tools help "primarily on easier targets", and weight access is not even among the tools tested ([AuditBench](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/auditbench)). Anthropic's investigator agent solves a hidden-objective game only **10–13%** of the time alone, or 42% when many runs are aggregated ([Anthropic](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/automated-auditing)). The closest work on reading weights for backdoors is Weight Oracles, which fine-tunes a 3B reader. It reaches AUROC **0.93** on a single attack and 0.81 on a four-attack mix, but only on one-layer transformers of about 2K parameters, and its authors name scaling as "the principal open challenge" ([Weight Oracles](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.07334v1)). A non-LLM method already does better at realistic scale. WeightWatch takes SVD directions of the fine-tune delta and flags **56–100%** of triggered completions at about 1% false-positive rate on 7–8B models. It needs the base weights and calibration activations, and shuffling hidden dimensions evades it ([WeightWatch](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00161v2)).

The opportunity on the read side is therefore labeling, not detection. An LLM can name a flagged ΔW direction, project it onto vocabulary and SAE features, and write probe texts to confirm it, as Exp 7b did. For narrow scalar prediction on model zoos, trained equivariant meta-networks reach Kendall τ ≈ **0.93** and are near saturation ([Small CNN Zoo leaderboard](https://www.wizwand.com/dataset/small-cnn-zoo); [NFN](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.14040)). A general LLM wins only on open-ended, cross-architecture questions where no labeled zoo exists, such as "what was this unknown network trained to do?"

## Writing networks from scratch is compilation, useful in narrow niches

Zero-shot policy writing is the most striking result in the post and the least useful one in practice. LLMs already write policies, reward functions and skill libraries as code. Code is easier to read, test and debug than 30K floats. Eureka's LLM-written reward code beat expert human rewards on **83% of 29** IsaacGym tasks ([Eureka](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12931)), and LLM-GS's LLM-seeded program search beats prior methods on almost all Karel tasks ([LLM-GS](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16450v3)). Astra itself works program-first: it reasons out the algorithm, then hand-encodes it into weights. Weaker LLMs fail on exactly that encoding step, through sign and scale errors ([yzw-22 repro](https://github.com/yzw-22/astra-weight-space-repro)). The compute argument is weak in the very environments the post used. Gymnax runs MinAtar Breakout at about 0.19 seconds per million steps on an A100 ([gymnax](https://github.com/RobertTLange/gymnax)). The "no environment feedback" framing also fails a key ablation. Without the game's source code, Astra's Seaquest network scores a median of **0.8**, which means the policies are compiled from the simulator rather than learned from play ([Zhang CLAIMS](https://github.com/ZhangYiqun018/rethinking-weight-space-programming/blob/main/results/CLAIMS.md)). Direct weight-writing earns its place only when the artifact must be a network: a fixed-shape policy server, vmapped JAX rollouts, an NPU or MCU runtime, or a differentiable component. Even then, "LLM writes Python → behavior cloning onto the network" is the obvious competitor, and nobody has compared the two.

Go confirms the ceiling. A 1.48M-weight net with layers written as programs (stones and liberties, groups in danger, move scoring) beats a 30-line tactics script 90–95% head-to-head ([Zhang CLAIMS](https://github.com/ZhangYiqun018/rethinking-weight-space-programming/blob/main/results/CLAIMS.md)). Its opponents, though, were PPO nets with 32-unit hidden layers. Gumbel AlphaZero trained for about 8 A100-hours beats Pachi with 10K simulations 62–38 ([Pgx](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.17503)). No search-free policy, hand-written or trained, competes with search plus self-play.

The "one-shot initialization" pitch is plausible, untested, and in tension with the plasticity literature. Program-derived weights can be excellent starting points: COMPNET-compiled surrogates were **1.91–9.50× more data-efficient** than random, MAML or pretrained inits ([Weber et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.15078)). Closed-form mimetic attention init speeds up ViT training ([Trockman & Kolter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09828)). Teacher policies help a lot as well. In reincarnating RL, QDagger students surpassed a 400M-frame teacher within 10M frames ([Agarwal et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.01626)). But Astra's policies encode decisions in saturated tanh regions ([yzw-22 repro](https://github.com/yzw-22/astra-weight-space-repro)), and its Breakout net has only 4% nonzero weights ([Zhang CLAIMS](https://github.com/ZhangYiqun018/rethinking-weight-space-programming/blob/main/results/CLAIMS.md)). Saturated, sparse weights pass almost no gradient. Plasticity loss can kill up to 90% of units in continual learning ([Dohare et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07711-7)), warm-started networks generalize worse than fresh ones ([Ash & Adams](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08475)), and fine-tuned pre-trained policies forget capabilities on states they stop visiting ([Wołczyk et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.02868)). Gradient training also compresses compiled structure rather than keeping it. Tracr's frac_prevs residual stream shrank from 14 to 6 dimensions without loss ([Tracr](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05062)), and compiled and trained models converge to similar feature counts ([arXiv 2512.13568](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.13568)). The safer design uses LLM-written networks as teachers through kickstarting, JSRL or QDagger, or writes the program into a residual branch while the rest of the network stays randomly initialized.

Replacing Adam is the least likely outcome. VeLO's learned optimizer took about 4,000 TPU-months of meta-training. An independent evaluation still found it needs problem-specific tuning and does not reliably beat its competitors ([Rezk et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.18191)), and a 2026 study reports it fails beyond 600M-parameter tasks ([arXiv 2602.19142](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2602.19142)). WSP sits at an extreme of the gradient-free family: very few evaluations, but each step is a frontier-model reasoning trace. Simplified evolution strategies with a population of just 30 already fine-tune full LLMs up to 14B and beat PPO and GRPO on Countdown ([Qiu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24372v2)). MeZO fine-tunes a 30B model on one A100 using only forward passes ([Malladi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17333)). LLM-in-the-loop code search has proven value at production scale, editing training code and leaving the weights to gradients. AlphaEvolve's kernel discoveries sped up a Gemini-training matmul by 23% and cut total training time by about 1% ([AlphaEvolve](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13131)). The winning hybrid is already visible in the 50-pass Go run, which amounts to a crude (1+1)-ES driven by LLM proposals. The LLM proposes structure (an init, a subspace, an edit, a curriculum) and ES, MeZO or Adam does the fitting.

Small-network niches remain where compilation earns its keep. Exact verification of ReLU networks is NP-complete ([Reluplex](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.01135)), so small, sparse, threshold-structured controllers are the networks verifiers can actually handle. That makes LLM-written controllers natural candidates for tinyML and certified runtimes, as long as a verifier checks them; the empirical keep-if-better loop guarantees nothing by itself. Generating ground-truth circuits for interpretability research and teaching is Tracr's stated purpose ([Tracr](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05062)). WSP delivers the same thing from an informal description, which makes it the lowest-risk, highest-value use today. In scientific ML, the role is encoding known physics as initial layers or debugging degenerate directions in trained surrogates. Neural operators get their value from fitting high-dimensional data, which WSP cannot do.

## Seven open problems decide whether the sparks catch

**Scale is the first wall.** The post itself calls 1.48M weights "too large for context" ([post](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/)), and Weight Oracles finds injecting full weights prohibitive beyond a few million parameters ([Weight Oracles](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.07334v1)). Above roughly a million parameters, WSP becomes interpretability-tool-driven editing: SVDs, projections and activation statistics. Its edge then depends entirely on choosing what to compute, and a human interpretability researcher has the same affordance. The second problem is **cost accounting**. The authors report no tokens, dollars or wall-clock time. The Qwen budget allowed up to 50 passes × 512 problems × 32,768 tokens ([post](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/)), which on a 0.8B model is comparable in target-model compute to a small LoRA run, before counting the frontier agent's own tokens. That estimate is mine, derived from the post's stated budget. The third is **process reproducibility**. One independent live re-run fell from 48% to 12.9% ([OpenWSP](https://github.com/openwsp/openwsp)), only 1 of 14 thinking-on runs produced the big gain, and fresh Seaquest replies scored a median of 33.5 against the published 53.1 ([Zhang CLAIMS](https://github.com/ZhangYiqun018/rethinking-weight-space-programming/blob/main/results/CLAIMS.md)). The field needs variance across sessions, not single selected runs. The authors' announced benchmark and harness had not been released as of these notes.

The fourth problem is **symmetry and superposition**. Hidden-unit permutations leave function unchanged but scramble raw weights, which is why trained weight-space learners build in equivariance ([NFN](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.14040)). An adversary can exploit the same symmetry to evade a weight-based detector ([WeightWatch](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00161v2)). The fifth is **verification**. Every claim a weight reader makes needs a forward pass to confirm: ablate the named head, patch the named direction. So "auditing without evaluation" can at most mean auditing without a behavioral eval suite. The sixth is **the missing baselines**. No one has compared LLM-written weights against LLM-written code plus behavior cloning, against PPO fine-tuning from an Astra init, against T2L/DnD on adaptation, or against ES or LoRA at a matched 50-evaluation budget on the looping task. Those four experiments would settle most verdicts in this report. The seventh is **the dual-use asymmetry**. Inserting a known circuit works better than inferring behavior from opaque weights, and that favors attackers over auditors who see only weights. A 2026 ACL paper already compiles activation steering into weights under null-space constraints to plant stealthy backdoors ([papernotes summary](https://en.papernotes.org/ACL2026/llm_safety/compiling_activation_steering_into_weights_via_null-space_constraints_for_stealt/)). HN commenters note that Exp 8 is the same technique community "abliteration" uses for decensoring ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50341174)). The tampering techniques were public before WSP. What WSP changes is that an agent can now apply them end to end from a goal.

## Conclusion

The post's claims are too broad, but it measured something real. A general-purpose model, used as it ships, can now select and execute the interpretability and editing recipes of the last five years end to end, and can compile informal algorithms into networks that open-weight models cannot write. That reframes WSP from "a new optimizer" to "an automated ML engineer whose output format happens to be tensors". It also predicts where the work goes next. Progress will come from harnesses that pair an LLM's structural proposals with cheap empirical fitting (ES, MeZO, behavior cloning, short fine-tunes) and activation-based verification. It will not come from LLMs reasoning over more raw floats. The capability boundary is information-theoretic rather than an engineering gap. An edit can only inject what the editor can state. A model can remove its own obstacles and graft known circuits, but new capability requires new information, and WSP gets that only through forward passes, the same bottleneck evolution strategies face. That boundary also cuts against the recursive self-improvement pitch.

For researchers choosing where to invest, the highest-return experiments are the head-to-head comparisons nobody has run. These are LLM weights versus LLM code plus behavior cloning, Astra-written inits under PPO fine-tuning, matched-budget ES or LoRA on the looping task, and agent versus difference-of-means on behaviors without an obvious contrast set. The most defensible near-term products are unglamorous. They include a repair assistant for small open models and deployed RL policies, a circuit generator for interpretability ground truth and teaching, and a ΔW-labeling tool inside activation-based audit agents. On the security side, the asymmetry deserves attention now. Writing circuits into networks already works better than reading behavior out of them, so integrity checks based on diffs and checksums matter more than auditing weight statistics. Edits touching 0.016% of weights can still change behavior drastically and push individual values by four orders of magnitude.
